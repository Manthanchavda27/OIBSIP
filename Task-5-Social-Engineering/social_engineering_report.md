# Research Report on Social Engineering Attacks

## Introduction

Social engineering attacks are cyberattacks that manipulate people into revealing confidential information or performing actions that compromise security. Instead of exploiting technical vulnerabilities, attackers exploit human psychology such as fear, trust, curiosity, or urgency.

---

# 1. Phishing Attack

## What is Phishing?

Phishing is a social engineering attack where attackers send fraudulent emails, messages, or websites pretending to be legitimate organizations in order to steal sensitive information such as passwords, banking details, or login credentials.

---

## How Phishing Works

Attackers create fake emails or websites that look genuine. Victims are tricked into:
- Clicking malicious links
- Downloading malware
- Entering passwords or financial details

---

## Impact of Phishing

- Identity theft
- Financial fraud
- Data breaches
- Malware infections

---

## Real-World Example

In 2016, attackers targeted Google and Facebook using fake invoices and phishing methods, resulting in financial losses exceeding $100 million.

---

## Prevention Methods

- User awareness training
- Email filtering
- Multi-factor authentication
- Verifying suspicious links
- Using anti-phishing tools

---

# 2. Pretexting Attack

## What is Pretexting?

Pretexting is a social engineering attack where an attacker creates a fabricated story or scenario to gain a victim’s trust and obtain sensitive information.

---

## How Pretexting Works

Attackers pretend to be:
- IT support staff
- Bank employees
- Government officials
- Company executives

The victim is manipulated into sharing confidential information.

---

## Impact of Pretexting

- Unauthorized access
- Data theft
- Financial loss
- Corporate espionage

---

## Real-World Example

Attackers often impersonate technical support teams to convince employees to reveal login credentials or security codes.

---

## Prevention Methods

- Identity verification procedures
- Security awareness training
- Limiting sensitive information sharing
- Employee education programs

---

# 3. Baiting Attack

## What is Baiting?

Baiting is a social engineering attack where attackers lure victims using attractive offers or infected physical media such as USB drives.

---

## How Baiting Works

Attackers leave infected USB drives or fake download links in public places. Victims connect the device or click the link, allowing malware to infect the system.

---

## Impact of Baiting

- Malware infection
- Data theft
- Ransomware attacks
- System compromise

---

## Real-World Example

Attackers have used infected USB devices labeled as “Salary Data” or “Confidential” to trick employees into connecting them to company computers.

---

## Prevention Methods

- Avoid unknown USB devices
- Disable autorun features
- Use endpoint protection
- Conduct cybersecurity training

---

# Importance of Security Awareness

Human error is one of the biggest causes of cybersecurity incidents. Organizations should regularly train employees to recognize suspicious emails, fake requests, and manipulation techniques.

---

# Conclusion

Social engineering attacks target human behavior rather than technical vulnerabilities. Attacks such as phishing, pretexting, and baiting can cause significant financial and reputational damage.

Organizations can reduce these risks through user education, security awareness programs, strong authentication methods, and proper cybersecurity policies.
